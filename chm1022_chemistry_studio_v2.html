Before you name it

  1. Separate the coordination sphere from counter-ions.
  2. Count donor atoms, not ligand molecules, for coordination number.
  3. Work out the metal oxidation state from the total charge.
  4. Identify ligand names, alphabetise them, then name the metal.

The operating manual

Study strategy

A deliberate sequence to turn a large syllabus into linked mental models. The aim is not to re-read everything; it is to repeatedly retrieve, draw, predict and justify.

How this studio teaches

The learning loop

1

Orient

Start with the chapter's one-sentence mental model and learning objectives. Identify what the structure or experiment is trying to explain.

2

Visualise

Rotate a model, inspect a mechanism, compare orbitals or read a spectrum. Say what changes and what stays conserved.

3

Work an example

Follow a fully worked example. Annotate every step with the rule that justifies it—not just the answer.

4

Retrieve on paper

Close the notes. Re-draw the structure, mechanism, spectrum interpretation or electron filling from memory in your notebook.

5

Interleave and explain

Mix old and new topics in the Exam Arena. Explain why the wrong alternatives are wrong.

Action plan by learning problem

Why each feature exists
Problem: invisible 3D structure

Build a spatial model

Use the molecule explorer, E/Z and R/S trainer, and coordination builder. Rotate or switch the representation; then sketch the same structure by hand. This prevents flat-page drawings from hiding geometry.

Success check: explain the geometry without looking at the diagram.
Problem: reaction arrows feel arbitrary

Follow electrons, not memorised steps

Use the mechanism theatre. On each step, identify the electron pair source, electron-poor target, leaving group and charge changes. Reconstruct the complete mechanism from memory afterwards.

Success check: every curved arrow starts at an electron pair or bond.
Problem: spectra seem like random peaks

Triangulate evidence

Use formula → IHD → IR → ¹H NMR → ¹³C NMR → MS as a constraint chain. A candidate must satisfy all data, not just one prominent peak.

Success check: justify each proposed feature with a signal and reject at least one alternative structure.
Problem: inorganic is too abstract

Connect charge, geometry and energy

Build the coordination sphere first; derive oxidation state and d count; then select geometry and fill split orbitals. Finish with unpaired electrons, magnetism and possible absorption.

Success check: solve the whole chain for a new metal–ligand complex.

How to use this guide responsibly

The chapter sequence and examinable topic coverage follow the 2026 outline you supplied. Monash's public handbook outcomes are used only as a broad cross-check. Moodle slides, tutorial solutions and your lecturer's conventions remain authoritative for exact assessment emphasis.

Some experimental data in the visual lab are schematic teaching examples. The model diagrams are conceptual, not to-scale molecular dynamics or experimentally recorded spectra. Use NIST or course-provided spectra when validating a real compound.

For best results, work with a notebook. For each concept: draw it, annotate the governing rule, solve one unfamiliar example and explain your answer aloud.
